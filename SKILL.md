# Brief Your Advocate

You do not need to install anything to try this once: copy this whole file, paste it as your first message in any AI chat tool, then follow it with your actual inputs.

An advocate carrying your case to someone else is doing work you cannot do directly, standing in a room or sending a message on your behalf. This prepares what they need to do that well. It does not replace them, contact anyone else directly, or assume what a further person cares about just because of their title.

## Gather the Inputs

- The evidence already established for your case: what has actually been confirmed, and what is still outstanding
- Who your advocate is actually presenting to or forwarding this to, and in what setting
- Anything already known about what each further person actually cares about, from something they or your advocate have said, not guessed from their role
- What is still unconfirmed, so it does not get accidentally presented as settled

## Map People to Evidence, Not to Titles

For each further person, record only what is actually known about what they care about. A role suggests an area of responsibility, not a confirmed priority. Where nothing is actually known about someone's concern beyond their title, say so, rather than inventing a plausible-sounding priority to fill the gap.

## Build the Briefing

1. **A summary for your advocate to speak from**, not a document to hand over unread: the ask, the evidence behind it, and the one or two points most likely to matter in this specific setting
2. **Person-specific points**, one short section per further person, using only what is actually known about their concern. Never repeat the same generic pitch under every name
3. **Likely questions, and an honest answer to each**, including "not yet confirmed" where that is genuinely the answer
4. **A message, if the situation calls for one**, drafted for your advocate to send in their own name, never addressed as though you were sending it directly

## Apply the Guardrails

- Never draft something addressed to a further person beyond your advocate; everything here is for your advocate to use, send, or present themselves
- Never assume someone's priority from their title alone; if nothing is actually known, say so
- Never resolve an outstanding item into a settled fact to make the case look stronger than it is
- Never invent a likely question; base it on what the evidence itself leaves open

## Stop When the Task Is Unsafe

Do not produce a briefing when:

- No advocate has actually agreed to carry this; preparing material for someone who has not agreed risks it being used without their real buy-in
- The request is to draft something addressed directly to a further person rather than material for your advocate
- The underlying evidence is too thin to say anything person-specific without guessing

## Require Human Review

Your advocate should check every point is something they are actually prepared to say themselves, and that nothing outstanding has been upgraded to settled. Sending any message, and presenting the case itself, stays with your advocate.
