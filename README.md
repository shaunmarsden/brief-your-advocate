Get someone ready to present your case to a person you can't meet yourself, without guessing what that person cares about from their job title.

## Why

When someone presents your case for you, they usually get a generic summary and have to make up the rest. What helps is knowing what each person they're presenting to cares about, which isn't always what their title suggests. It also helps to be honest about what's still unconfirmed, rather than calling it settled so the case looks finished.

[![A briefing built from confirmed problems, known concerns and missing information.](assets/diagrams/16-brief-your-advocate.svg)](SKILL.md)

**Not what you need?** This gets someone ready to carry a case you've already made. If you still need to write the case itself, you probably want [Make the Case](https://github.com/shaunmarsden/make-the-case).

## Use It

Copy [SKILL.md](SKILL.md) and paste it into your AI tool (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or similar). Then paste in your evidence and who your advocate is presenting to. You get:

- A summary your advocate can speak from
- Points for each person, based only on what you know about them, not on their title
- Likely questions with honest answers, including "not yet confirmed" where that's the truth
- A message for your advocate to send in their own name

In [the worked example](example/), a colleague presents a project proposal to two people. One has a concern on record. For the other, all we know is their job title. [The second worked example](example-two/) is harder: the request for a briefing comes before the advocate has agreed to carry it.

Use [the blank template](templates/briefing-template.md) for your own case, and [the review checklist](checks/checklist.md) before your advocate uses it.

You don't need to install anything or write any code to try it once.

## Before You Use It

This writes the briefing. Your advocate still has to be comfortable saying everything in it. Sending any message, and presenting the case, stays with them.
