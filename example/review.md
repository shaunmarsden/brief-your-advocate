# Review: Jordan's Briefing

I checked [output.md](output.md) against what [inputs.md](inputs.md) was built to test.

## What Worked

It based Priya's points on what she said, not on her title. Her concern about maintenance came from a comment in a corridor. The output led with that for her, rather than repeating a general pitch about time saved under her name.

It didn't guess at Tom's concern. Nobody knows what Tom cares about. There's only an assumption from his title: a Finance Director probably cares about cost. The output said so. It didn't invent a likely-sounding budget objection and answer a question Tom may never ask.

It left the hosting cost unknown. It would have been easy to estimate a number to make Jordan look better prepared. The output said "not yet confirmed" in the summary, the likely questions and the draft message.

It wrote the draft message for Jordan to send in their own name, not as though you were sending it.

## What Still Needs a Human Check

Before the meeting, Jordan should check they're comfortable saying everything in the "Person-Specific Points" section. Being handed lines isn't the same as being ready to say them when someone asks questions.

If Tom raises a specific concern in the meeting, this briefing can't have foreseen it. Jordan will have to handle it on the spot, and shouldn't expect the document to cover everything.

## Verdict

No automatic failure. The briefing told a known concern (Priya's) apart from an assumed one (Tom's). It also refused to turn the one outstanding item into a number just to look finished.
