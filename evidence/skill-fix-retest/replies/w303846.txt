# Briefing for Oriel: moving the weekly all-hands to fortnightly

## Summary to speak from

**The ask:** Take the proposal to move the all-hands from weekly to fortnightly to the leadership meeting, for a decision.

**What is confirmed:**
- The all-hands takes one hour every Monday for about 60 people. This comes from the calendar invite and the attendance list.
- The survey went to 30 people. 12 answered, and 9 of those 12 prefer fortnightly. That is a view from 9 of 12 respondents, not from the whole group. Nobody else has been asked.

**What is not yet settled:**
- Whether a fortnightly meeting would need to be longer than an hour.
- What the 18 people who did not answer think, and what the wider group of about 60 think.

**The points most likely to matter in this room:**
- Elspeth has said the all-hands is the only place she hears directly from frontline staff, and she does not want to lose that. Lead with how the proposal keeps that channel, not with time saved. We do not yet know how a fortnightly format would affect it, so say that plainly.
- The survey is small. Say so before anyone else does.

## Elspeth Halvorsen, Operations Director

- **Known:** She values hearing directly from frontline staff at the all-hands and does not want to lose that. She said so at the last leadership meeting.
- **Not known:** How she would weigh a fortnightly format against that, or whether she has a view on meeting length.
- **Suggested approach:** Ask her what would need to be true for a fortnightly all-hands to keep that direct channel.

## Ruairi Pargeter, Head of Communications

- **Known:** Nothing about his view on this proposal.
- **Suggested approach:** Do not assume his priorities from his role. If you can, ask him for his view before the meeting. If not, say at the meeting that his view is not yet known.

## Likely questions and honest answers

1. **How many people want this?** 9 of the 12 who answered the survey prefer fortnightly. Only 12 of the 30 people surveyed answered, and nobody else has been asked.
2. **Would the fortnightly meeting be longer?** That has not been decided.
3. **Would frontline staff still hear directly from leadership, and get to speak?** Not yet confirmed. The proposal has not been tested against this.
4. **How much time would it save?** If the meeting stays one hour, fortnightly halves the time spent, from one hour a week to one hour every two weeks, for about 60 people. This is arithmetic from the proposal, not a measured result.
5. **Has the wider group been consulted?** No. Only the 30 people surveyed have been asked.

## Message

No message is drafted. The proposal is to be presented in person at the leadership meeting. Check that every point above is something you are prepared to say yourself, and that nothing still open is presented as settled.
