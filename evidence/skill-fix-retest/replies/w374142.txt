# Briefing for Oriel Featherstone: moving the all-hands to fortnightly

## 1. What to speak from

**The ask:** Leadership agrees to change the weekly all-hands to fortnightly.

**What is confirmed:**
- The all-hands runs for one hour every Monday with about 60 people. This comes from the calendar invite and the attendance list.
- A short survey went to 30 people. 12 answered, and 9 of those 12 said they would prefer fortnightly. Nobody else has been asked. Say this plainly: it is a small sample, not a company view.

**What is not yet known:**
- Whether a fortnightly all-hands would be longer than an hour. This has not been decided. Do not say it will be the same length or shorter.
- What the 18 people who did not answer think, and what everyone else thinks.

**The points most likely to matter in this room:**
- Elspeth has said the all-hands is the one place she hears directly from frontline staff, and she does not want to lose that. Fortnightly halves how often that happens. The proposal does not yet say how that gap would be covered. Say so rather than implying it is handled.
- The time saving is real if the meeting stays an hour: roughly 60 person-hours a week becomes about 30 on average. This only holds if the length stays the same, which is still open.

## 2. Points for each person

**Elspeth Halvorsen, Operations Director**
- Known concern, from her own words at the last leadership meeting: keeping direct contact with frontline staff.
- Lead with what happens to frontline voice under a fortnightly cadence. If there is no answer yet, say that, and offer to take it away as an action. Do not present a replacement that has not been agreed.

**Ruairi Pargeter, Head of Communications**
- Nothing is known about his view on this. Do not assume he cares about how often staff hear from leadership, or about anything else, because of his role.
- Before the meeting, ask him directly what he would need to see to support or oppose the change. If that is not possible, present the evidence and ask him what he would want to know.

## 3. Likely questions, with honest answers

- **How many people asked for this?** 30 were surveyed, 12 replied, and 9 of those preferred fortnightly. No one else has been asked.
- **Will the fortnightly meeting be longer?** Not decided yet.
- **What happens to frontline voice between meetings?** Not yet addressed in the proposal.
- **Does the survey represent everyone?** No. It reached 30 people and 12 answered.
- **Is the time saving confirmed?** Only if the meeting stays one hour. Length is still open.
