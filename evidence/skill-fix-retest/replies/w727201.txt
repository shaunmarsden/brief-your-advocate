# Briefing for Oriel Featherstone

## Summary to Speak From

**The ask:** move the weekly all-hands to fortnightly.

**The evidence behind it:**

- The all-hands takes an hour every Monday for about 60 people, which is roughly 60 person-hours a week. That comes from the calendar invite and the attendance list.
- A short survey went to 30 people. 12 answered, and 9 of those 12 said they'd prefer fortnightly. That is 9 people out of about 60 who attend. It is a signal in favour, not a mandate. The other 18 people who got the survey didn't answer, and the other 30 or so attendees weren't asked.

**What's outstanding:** nobody has decided whether a fortnightly all-hands would need to run longer than an hour. Until that's settled, the time saving can't be stated as a figure. If it stays at an hour, it halves the time spent. If it grows, the saving shrinks.

**The point most likely to matter in this room:** Elspeth has already said the all-hands is the only place she hears directly from frontline staff. Be ready to talk about that. If you can, make it part of the proposal rather than treating it as an objection to get past.

## Person-Specific Points

**For Elspeth Halvorsen (Operations Director):** At the last leadership meeting she said the all-hands is the only place she hears directly from frontline staff, and she doesn't want to lose that. Nothing in the current evidence speaks to her concern. The survey doesn't tell us whether frontline staff responded, or how they feel about fortnightly. Don't claim fortnightly protects frontline contact. The honest position is that it halves how often that contact happens, unless something else fills the gap. If you want to offer a way to keep it, put it forward as an open question for the group, not as part of the agreed plan. For example, a standing slot for frontline input in each fortnightly session.

**For Ruairi Pargeter (Head of Communications):** Nothing is known about his view on this. Don't assume his priorities from his title. It may be worth asking him directly in the meeting what he'd need to see.
