# Briefing for Oriel: moving the all-hands to fortnightly

Oriel has agreed to present this, so the briefing is below. It's written for Oriel to speak from at the leadership meeting, not as a document to hand round.

---

## 1. What to say

**The ask:** move the Monday all-hands from weekly to fortnightly.

**What's confirmed:**
- It runs for an hour every Monday with about 60 people. That's around 60 person-hours a week (source: the calendar invite and the attendance list).
- A short survey went to 30 people. 12 replied, and 9 of those 12 said they'd prefer fortnightly.

**What isn't settled yet:**
- Whether a fortnightly all-hands would need to run longer than an hour. Nobody has decided this, so the time saving can't be given as a fixed number. If it stayed at an hour, it would halve. If it got longer, the saving would be smaller.
- What the other 48 people think. The survey went to half the attendees, and only 12 people answered it.

**The point most likely to matter in this room:** Elspeth has already said the all-hands is the only place she hears directly from frontline staff. Raise that yourself before anyone else does. Don't wait for it to come up as an objection.

**How to present the survey fairly:** "9 of the 12 who replied prefer fortnightly." Don't call it "75% of staff" or "most people". 12 replies from about 60 attendees shows which way some people lean. It isn't a mandate.

---

## 2. Points for each person

### Elspeth Halvorsen, Operations Director
**What we know:** at the last leadership meeting she said the all-hands is the only place she hears directly from frontline staff, and she doesn't want to lose that.

**What to say:**
- Acknowledge her concern directly. A fortnightly meeting means half as many chances to hear from frontline staff. Don't play that down.
- Be honest about what the proposal covers. It doesn't yet include anything to replace that contact. No alternative has been proposed or agreed.
- Be honest about the survey too. We don't know whether frontline staff were among the 30 people asked or the 12 who replied, so it can't be said to reflect their view.
- One fair way to handle it: ask her what she'd need from the fortnightly format, or alongside it, to keep that contact. That treats her concern as something the decision has to account for, not an objection to argue past.

### Ruairi Pargeter, Head of Communications
**What we know:** nothing about his view on this.

**What to say:**
- Nothing tailored. His job title doesn't tell us what he'll care about, and guessing would mean presenting an assumption as his concern.
- Give him the same summary as everyone else, and if there's a natural moment, ask him what he thinks. His answer is worth more than any guess made beforehand.

## 3. Likely questions and honest answers

These come from the gaps in the evidence itself.

**"How many people actually want this?"**
9 of the 12 who replied to the survey. The survey went to 30 of the roughly 60 attendees. The other 30 weren't asked, and 18 of those who were asked didn't reply. So we have a lean from a small group, not a staff-wide view.

**"Who was surveyed? Does it include frontline staff?"**
I don't know. Don't guess here. Say it's not confirmed and that it can be checked.

**"Would the fortnightly meeting be longer?"**
Not decided yet. That's an open question the decision would need to settle.

**"So how much time does this actually save?"**
It depends on the length question. At an hour it goes from about 60 person-hours a week to about 30 a week on average. If the meeting gets longer, the saving shrinks. There's no confirmed figure yet.

**"How do we keep the frontline contact?"** (most likely from Elspeth)
The proposal doesn't answer that yet. Nothing has been proposed to replace it. It's a fair condition to set on the decision.

**"Could we survey everyone before deciding?"**
Yes. The other 30 attendees haven't been asked, so that's an open option. Whether to do it is for the meeting to decide.
